The framework

PRISM: five stages, run in order

Run the five stages once, then return to whichever stage is holding your practice back.

  1. P

    Position

    Choose a market, build an offer and price it to compete on value, not hours.

  2. R

    Reach

    Find qualified prospects, personalise outreach and move conversations towards signed clients.

  3. I

    Implement

    Deliver strategy, content, ads, email and search work that AI accelerates but does not replace.

  4. S

    Systemize

    Turn one-off delivery into onboarding, reporting, SOPs and light, reviewed automation.

  5. M

    Master

    Quality-control every AI output, work ethically and scale only when the system can support it.

In practice PRISM runs as eleven working stages: Position, Reach, Qualify, Sell, Onboard, Deliver, Report, Retain, Expand, Systemize and Scale.

How the prompts are built

Example excerpt, shortened from the “Niche and Market Opportunity Analysis” prompt. The full prompt and 23 others are in the ebook.

Role
A market research strategist who specialises in defensible service niches for independent marketing consultants.
Objective
Evaluate whether [proposed niche] is viable, and suggest two adjacent alternatives if it is weak.
Constraints
Do not invent market-size statistics or cite studies you cannot name accurately…
Questions first
If minimum project size or constraints are missing, ask before proceeding.

What the preview shows

Prompts are templates with bracketed placeholders you fill with real information. Each states constraints, not just goals, and asks for missing details rather than guessing.

Fictional examples in the ebook are labelled, and it advises against fabricating testimonials, statistics or results.
